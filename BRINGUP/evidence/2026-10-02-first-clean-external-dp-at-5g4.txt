## Still unknown / next

- Hotplug at 5.4 Gbps (unplug/replug) has not been tested: every earlier hotplug
  failure was at 8.1 Gbps.
- The MSI (5120x1440) has not been tried at 5.4 Gbps. Its 60 Hz mode fits inside
  HBR2 x4 (~10.6 Gbps raw vs 21.6 Gbps); 120 Hz would not.
- The first boot after staging `dprate` came up to a black screen after login and
  needed a second reboot. No kernel error was recorded for that boot (its journal
  was truncated by the hard reset); treat it as open, not as explained.
- Permanent form of the fix: either make the external-DP rate selection respect the
  sink's basic `MAX_LINK_RATE` (DPCD 0x00001) instead of the extended receiver
  capability block the kernel merges over it, or implement the missing fallback so
  a failed LTTPR/sink channel equalization down-shifts the rate and retries. The
  8.1 Gbps capability is real per the sinks' extended blocks; this platform simply
  does not train it reliably, so the QMP/PHY side at HBR3 remains a separate open
  question for upstream.

    *ERROR* link training of LTTPR(s) failed. ret=-110

and then, on the way out:

    [drm:dpu_encoder_phys_vid_disable] *ERROR* wait disable failed: id:38 intf:4 ret:-110

The failed encoder cannot even be disabled, which is why the pipeline stays wedged
and the desktop freezes rather than merely losing the external picture. The monitor's
own USB side came up normally (VIA `2109:2211` hub, `1462:3fa4` MSI Gaming Controller,
`MSI Optix Driver` mass storage), so its hub/PD is fine - it is the DP repeater
segment that never equalizes.

Summary of every MSI configuration tried on this machine: DP-1 at 8.1G (150232),
DP-2 at 8.1G (163318), DP-2 at 5.4G (this one) - all fail the same way. The Gigabyte
on DP-1 at 5.4G is the one working combination.

## The "first boot after a freeze is black after login" pattern

Observed twice: after a freeze caused by this failure, the first boot reaches the
login screen, the user logs in, the screen refreshes once and never comes back; the
second boot is normal.

Likely mechanism, and it fits the driver logs: module parameters do not persist, so
every boot starts with no cap (8.1G). If a failing monitor is attached at that boot,
the same failure happens again - and GNOME applies its saved layout immediately after
login, i.e. exactly when the broken external encoder is part of the atomic commit,
so the commit waits on a vblank that never comes and the internal panel goes dark
while the session stays alive. That is the documented dark-panel-after-login
signature (`dp_aux_backlight` powered down, `card1-eDP-1 enabled=disabled`, session
alive), whose known remedy is restarting gdm rather than rebooting. Booting with the
monitor absent (or a working monitor with the cap in place) comes up normally.
The journal of the first such boot is truncated by the hard reset, so it carries no
direct evidence - the mechanism above is inference, not measurement.
