Findings
--------
1. Suspend/resume itself works in s2idle with the lid: 34 s asleep and the machine came back with
   no hang. The display came back (the system-sleep hook), the radio did not.

2. a16_keep_mhi_up=0 does NOT keep the radio. The resume waits for the firmware and expires with
   -110 (ETIMEDOUT) -- the same signature the unpatched kernel had -- so the re-attach path did
   not complete either. Neither of our two resume paths (keep-MHI-up, re-attach) survives.

3. It is not ath12k-specific: dwc3-qcom a600000.usb failed to resume with -110 in the same resume
   pass. Two independent devices timing out points at a platform-level resume problem
   (interconnect/NoC, power domains or clock state) rather than the Wi-Fi driver alone. This is
   the first time a non-Wi-Fi device has shown the same failure.

4. The Wi-Fi hardware is healthy afterwards:
     0004:01:00.0  vendor 0x17cb  device 0x1112  current_link_speed 8.0 GT/s  width x1
                   power_state D0  enable 1  driver ath12k_wifi7_pci bound
                   netdev wlP4p1s0 present, rfkill 6 entries
   So the PCIe link and the device survived; what is wedged is the firmware/driver state, not the
   hardware. (Read with plain user rights; no root needed.)

5. There is no reboot-free recovery from that state, and this is already measured -- reload_wifi's
   ladder: rung 1 (NetworkManager) and rung 2 (supplicant) no effect; rung 3 unbind/bind -- the
   unbind succeeds, the bind does not return the radio; hard rung h3 (PCI function remove + bus
   rescan) and h4 (module unload) both go through the same remove path, and h4 froze the whole
   machine (hard reset). A wedged radio therefore costs a reboot, which is why the fix has to be
   the resume path and not the recovery ladder.

6. Leading suspect for finding 3: the platform's clock and interconnect providers never finish
   sync_state(), so the NoC and clock controllers are left un-settled:

     qnoc-glymur interconnect-1: sync_state() pending due to 1dfa000.crypto
     qnoc-glymur 16e0000.interconnect: sync_state() pending due to 1dfa000.crypto
     gcc-glymur 100000.clock-controller: sync_state() pending due to 3d6c000.gmu
     gpucc-glymur 3d90000.clock-controller: sync_state() pending due to 3d6c000.gmu

   Two consumers -- the crypto engine (QCE, 1dfa000.crypto) and the GPU management unit
   (3d6c000.gmu) -- never probe, so their providers never settle. A device whose interconnect or
   clocks are not managed can time out exactly like this at resume. Worth chasing as the common
   cause of both the ath12k and the dwc3 failures.

7. Separately observed (not investigated): after the resume the display sits in a pm_runtime
   storm -- msm_dp_pm_runtime_resume / msm_dp_pm_runtime_suspend repeating dozens of times per
   second (23:28:54, type=10, core_init=0 phy_init=0).
